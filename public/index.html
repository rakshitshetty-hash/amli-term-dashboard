How these numbers are built
  • Leads come from the unique lead dump, dated by lead creation date. Sales and APE come from the Direct & D2C sales dump, dated by payment date. Spend comes from the Daily Spends Tracker (Term All Channels).
  • Line items follow the Master Tracker mapping. NewBlank7 sits in Brand. Bottom Funnel = Brand + Core + Others. Display = PR + RM. On this tab, Netcore includes the cross-sell and upsell journeys, as in the plan; the Day on day tab shows the Netcore line item alone, against its own plan rows.
  • Targets are the Nov'26 media plan. Month-to-date and daily targets follow the plan's daily phasing (QL curve for leads, NOP and APE curves for sales and APE), not a straight split by days.
  • Total sales and APE targets use the plan's unique-sale figures (8,688 sales, ₹25.49 Cr APE). Group targets add up to more because some sales count in more than one group.
  • CVR divides sales by payment date by leads by creation date, so the latest days read low until those leads pay.
  • Cream benchmarks are each line item's best day in August 2026. A day counts only with at least 50 leads and 30% of that line item's typical daily volume, and a share far above the rest of the month (above Q3 + 1.5 × IQR) is treated as an anomaly.
  • "Furthest behind" looks only at groups with a month APE target of ₹100 L or more.

              Trend

              What the report says · 14 Oct – 12 Nov

              Structural findings from the first 30 days of this report, written on 13 Nov. The flags above track them day by day.

              Leakage

              The 7–9 pm dead zone

              12% of leads arrive between 7 and 9 pm. On a normal weekday 14% of them are never dialled, about 230 leads a day, against 2–3% at every other hour. Leads after 9 pm roll into the next day's queue and get dialled; 7–9 pm leads don't. Fix: move the business-date cut-off to 7 pm, or keep a late shift until 9 pm.

              Leakage

              1 in 10 leads is closed without a single dial

              10.6% of leads close with no dial attempt, 15–25% in Bottom Funnel (24.6% on 12 Nov). These are likely duplicates, existing customers or auto-closures, but the report doesn't say. Ask for a closure reason; if they are dupes, the media lead count is overstated by the same share.

              Capacity

              Vernac is falling behind

              Since 8 Nov, 7–10% of Vernac leads (about 270 a day) stay open and undialled, against under 1% on 3–5 Nov. Agents fell from 58–59 to 44–54 while leads held at 2.7–3.5K, and calls per agent dropped from 320–380 to 265–290 on 9–11 Nov. Vernac also has the longest talk time (3.1 min) and lowest connect (16.5%), so it needs the most agent time per lead.

              Capacity

              SEM Core is overstaffed

              SEM Core runs a 28% idle rate (others 12–17%) on 42 leads per agent (others about 55). When it ran 14–16 agents on 10–12 Nov, idle fell to 15–23% with no extra undialled leads. About 4 agents could move to Vernac.

              Productivity

              More dials, lower connect

              Across segments, days with more calls per agent have lower Connect% (correlation −0.7 overall, −0.78 Affiliate). Extra dials go into re-dialling the same unreachable pool. Track connects per agent (about 58 a day) rather than calls per agent as the productivity number.

              Productivity

              Bottom Funnel wrap time is heavy

              Bottom Funnel agents spend 164 s wrapping each connected call, against 89 s in Vernac and 96 s in Affiliate: about 2.2 hours of an 8.7-hour login. Cutting it to the overall 115 s frees roughly 40 minutes per agent per day, worth about 4–5 more connects per agent at their current pace.

              Quality

              Affiliate connects most, talks least

              Affiliate has the best Connect% (24%) but the shortest talk time (1.8 min against 2.8 in Bottom Funnel). Quick connects and short calls point to low-intent or wrong-number leads. Worth checking against Affiliate CVR on the tracker.

              Staffing

              Sundays run 20% lighter

              Sundays average 207 agents against 252–267 on other days, and undialled leads jump (15% on Sun 8 Nov, 22% in Top Funnel). 7 Nov looks like a dialer or system outage: logins were normal but calls per agent fell 60% and idle rose to 44% in every segment.

              How the numbers are built, and gaps in the report
              • Agent times are hours per agent. Ready time = login − not-ready. True utilisation = (talk + dial) ÷ ready time, and idle rate = idle ÷ ready time; both match the report's own columns. Connects per agent = calls per agent × Connect%.
              • Lead dialling uses the hourly tabs: lead counts by creation hour, business date, status (open or closed) and dial status. Leads created after 9 pm count in the next business date.
              • The latest day's undialled share reads high until that day's leads have been worked.
              • Segment agents add up to about 85% of Overall, so about 15% of agents sit outside these five segments.
              • Wrap time per connected call on 30 Oct (37 s, against about 115 s) looks like a reporting error in every segment.
              • What would sharpen this: call dispositions (interested, callback, not eligible), time from lead creation to first dial, the reason a lead closed without a dial, and a lead ID to join with the sales dump for CVR by segment.

                      Trend

                      What the report says · 14 Oct – 12 Nov

                      Structural findings from the first 30 days, written on 13 Nov. Cross-checked against the sales and call-centre data on this dashboard.

                      Trend

                      Success is up 4 pts, all from UPI

                      Success rose from 75.7% (14–27 Oct) to 80.1% (29 Oct – 12 Nov). Breaking it down, the shift in payment mix added nothing; all of the gain is UPI working better (74% → 80%). UPI peaked at 86% on 1 Nov and has slipped back to about 78% since, so it's worth asking whether a gateway or app change happened around 20 Oct and what changed after 1 Nov.

                      Leakage

                      UPI is 79% of all failed payments

                      About 80 payment attempts fail a day, 60 of them on UPI. Every point of UPI success is worth about 3 more successful payments a day. Failure reasons by UPI app (GPay, PhonePe, Paytm) and by error code would show whether it's user drop-off, bank declines or timeouts.

                      Segment

                      PR has the weakest checkout, and it's UPI

                      PR succeeds 72.8% of the time against 86.3% for SEO. The whole gap is UPI (71.5% vs 86.1%); PR card payments succeed 90%. PR traffic is mostly Meta, and UPI app hand-off often breaks inside the Facebook and Instagram in-app browser. Worth testing a UPI QR or collect fallback, or opening checkout in the external browser. Matching SEO's UPI rate adds about 7 PR payments a day.

                      Leakage

                      Debit card and net banking fail half the time

                      Debit card succeeds 50% and net banking 46%. They're 4% of attempts but 10% of failures. Typical causes are OTP or 3-D Secure drop-off and bank-side declines. Check bank-level failure codes before deciding whether to push these users to UPI or credit card.

                      Mix

                      Credit card share is rising, and card works

                      Credit card went from 14% of attempts (week of 12 Oct) to 23% (week of 9 Nov), and it succeeds 87% of the time. For larger premiums, showing card or EMI first on the payment page could lift success further.

                      Link

                      Payments follow the call centre

                      Daily payment attempts move with call-centre connects (correlation 0.76). On 7–8 Nov, the dialer outage day and a half-staffed Sunday, attempts fell to 235–244 from 370–456 and successful payments dropped about 40%. Many payments are agent-assisted, so call-centre outages show up here the same day.

                      Coverage

                      The report covers about 58% of tracker sales

                      For 1–11 Nov it shows 3,071 successful payments against 5,333 sales on the tracker. Coverage is 86% for Core, 85% for PR and 65% for SEO. It probably counts only online checkout payments; worth confirming which journeys are in it before using it as a sales proxy.

                      Data

                      Gaps to close with the client

                      "Transactions" are attempts, so retries count as failures; a unique-customer success rate would show how many buyers are actually lost. Segment tabs run a day behind Overall (12 Nov shows 11 transactions against 400). About 70% of volume sits outside Core, PR and SEO; Brand, RM and Affiliate tabs would close that.

                      How the numbers are built
                      • Successful payments = transactions × success %, and the same for each method. Method attempts add up to total transactions, and method successes add up to total successes, on every row.
                      • Card = credit card. Debit + NB combines debit card and net banking, which are too small to read alone.
                      • A segment's day is marked incomplete when its transactions are under 30% of its usual, as on 12 Nov; flags skip it.
